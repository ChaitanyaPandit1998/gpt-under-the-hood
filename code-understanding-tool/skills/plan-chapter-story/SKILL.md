# Plan a Chapter Story

Identify one teaching goal from the selected chapter and supplied source. Choose
a concrete analogy whose actions explain the mechanism, relationship or
limitation, rather than merely replacing technical nouns with characters.
Honor supplied audience, depth and relevant user preferences without changing
the factual claim to fit a preferred storyline.

Plan a setup, one to five connected scenes, and a takeaway. Keep the setting,
characters, objects and rules consistent. Each scene should advance the same
lesson; do not invent conflict, actors or extra scenes just to fill the limit.

Map each important narrative action to its technical meaning. Check direction,
ordering, quantities, domains and conditional outcomes. Name where the analogy
stops matching. If the analogy cannot preserve the essential relationship,
choose another analogy or state the limitation instead of hiding the mismatch.

Separate source-supported claims, established background and illustrative
fiction. Preserve uncertainty, missing context, and distinctions between
intended and observed behavior. For a PR, distinguish old from new behavior
and stay within supplied evidence. A story outcome cannot establish a proof,
runtime observation or actual measurement.

The application may combine planning with writing in one call. In that case,
use the plan to guide the story and return only the requested story schema;
do not add outline fields or expose internal planning notes.
